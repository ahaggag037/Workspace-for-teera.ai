# Current State — Hypotheses, Open Questions & Research Debt

## Central hypothesis
**SPECULATIVE.** A reusable model-independent cognitive/operational layer may measurably improve different compatible AI models in reliability, evidence use, continuity, safety, and auditability. No controlled multi-model evidence has yet established stable uplift.

## Quarantined historical component-shaped labels
These are preserved as research history, not settled architecture: `Cognitive Control Plane`, `Executive Control Layer`, `Typed Memory`, `Model Gateway`, `Knowledge Layer`.

## Competing framings to test
- **F1 — Containment / blast radius:** reliability means limiting harm when models fail.
- **F2 — Capability absorption:** scaffolding may become obsolete as frontier models improve.
- **F3 — Governance / accountability first:** enterprise trust may depend more on responsibility and auditability than added cognition.
- **F4 — Reliability as economics:** reliable outcomes per unit cost/latency may be the real target.
- **F5 — Verifiable environments first:** making environments/actions more verifiable may matter more than external cognition.

## Highest-information unknowns
1. Which production failure modes disappear with stronger models, and which remain systems/security/governance problems regardless of model quality?
2. Does an external layer provide measurable uplift on the same model under controlled conditions?
3. Which external primitives remain valuable across multiple model generations rather than becoming scaffolding debt?
4. Can instruction/prompt injection be structurally contained, or must it remain a permanent risk-management problem?
5. What operational definition of acceptable agent failure will enterprises, regulators, or insurers converge on?
6. What is the cost of verification, recovery, and oversight relative to generation as task length grows?
7. Is universal state/memory ontology realistic, or must reliability be strongly domain-specific?

## Research debt
- No controlled experiment on the central hypothesis.
- Production-incident corpus not yet built.
- Capability-trend literature such as METR needs continuous apples-to-apples tracking.
- Vendor evidence is overrepresented relative to independent academic/standards/incident evidence.
- Simplicity principle remains in tension with the expanding list of candidate concerns.
- No project-specific threat model yet.
- Negative-result logging has been weak.
- Several historical citations/dates/links need retroactive cleanup.

## Current research frontier
**What actually fails in deployed AI-agent systems, and what fraction of those failures is eliminated by stronger models versus remaining a systems/security/governance problem?**
